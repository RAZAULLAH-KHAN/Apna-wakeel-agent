# Practical Legal Guidance Manual — Pakistan Scenarios

## Scenario 1: Police Station Night Summons or Call Without an FIR
**Category: police**
*Common situation:* You receive a late-night phone call or WhatsApp message from a police sub-inspector commanding you: "Come to the police station right now."
*Stay calm rule:* Police cannot summon you informally at night over the phone without written notice.
*Your legal rights:*
1. Under Section 160 CrPC, any police summons for questioning must be an official written notice.
2. Under Section 154 CrPC, formal investigation requires a registered FIR.
3. Under Article 10 of the Constitution of Pakistan 1973, you have a right to know the grounds of any accusation and consult an advocate.
*What to do right now:*
1. Note the officer's name, belt number, and police station name.
2. Ask respectfully: "Is there an FIR registered? What is the FIR number, date, and sections?"
3. Offer to appear during daylight working hours (10:00 AM) accompanied by your lawyer or family elders.
*Exact script to say:*
"Janab, please confirm the FIR number in writing. Under Section 160 CrPC, a formal written summons is required. I will appear tomorrow morning in daylight hours accompanied by my legal counsel."

## Scenario 2: Street Vendor, Shopkeeper or Business Facing Bribery or Extortion
**Category: business**
*Common situation:* A patrol officer or municipal inspector demands "chai pani" cash or threatens to seal your shop.
*Your legal rights:*
1. Demanding illegal gratification by a public servant is a crime under Section 161 PPC and Section 5(2) Prevention of Corruption Act 1947.
2. Threatening harm to property to extract money is criminal extortion under Section 383/384 PPC.
*What to do right now:*
1. Ask politely for the official government challan/receipt voucher.
2. Note officer badge number, vehicle plate, and location.
3. Report to Senior Superintendent of Police (SSP) Complaints Cell or Anti-Corruption Helpline 111-622-622.
*Exact script to say:*
"Officer, if there is a violation, please issue the official government treasury challan so I can deposit the fine in the National Bank. I cannot give cash without an official voucher."

## Scenario 3: Harassment, Stalking, or Physical Intimidation of Women
**Category: women**
*Common situation:* A woman or student is being followed, catcalled, or threatened in public, workplace, or transit.
*Your legal rights:*
1. Section 509 PPC makes any word, sound, gesture, or act insulting the modesty of a woman punishable with up to 3 years imprisonment.
2. Section 506 PPC punishes criminal intimidation with up to 7 years imprisonment.
*What to do right now:*
1. Move to a safe, well-lit populated area immediately.
2. Call Women Protection Helpline 1043 (Punjab) / 1094 (Sindh) or Police 15.
3. Preserve evidence: note timestamps, descriptions, vehicle license plates.
*Exact script to say:*
"Stop this immediately. Your action is a criminal offence under Section 509 of the Pakistan Penal Code. I am recording your details and calling Police Helpline 15 right now."

## Scenario 4: Online Blackmail, Cyber Extortion, and Private Photo Leakage
**Category: cyber**
*Common situation:* An individual threatens to leak personal photos, chats, or videos unless money is transferred.
*Stay calm rule:* First, do NOT panic and NEVER PAY. Paying never stops a blackmailer; it only leads to escalating demands.
*Your legal rights:*
1. Under Section 21 of PECA 2016, blackmailing with private images/videos carries up to 5 years imprisonment and 5 million PKR fine.
2. Under Section 24 of PECA 2016, cyberstalking and persistent coercion is a criminal offense.
*What to do right now:*
1. Do not pay any money.
2. Take full screenshots showing perpetrator's phone number, profile link, and threat messages before they unsend.
3. Report immediately to FIA Cyber Crime Wing via helpline 1991 or portal (complaint.fia.gov.pk).
*Exact script to say:*
"I will not pay you any money. Your conduct violates Section 21 and Section 24 of the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA 2016). All your messages, numbers, and account details have been captured and submitted to the FIA Cyber Crime Wing."

## Scenario 5: Physical Assault, Road Rage, or Slap on the Street
**Category: assault**
*Common situation:* A motorist, pedestrian, or individual physically attacks you, slaps you, or uses violent force on the road.
*Stay calm rule:* Do not engage in retaliatory physical violence that could endanger your life. Move to a safe place.
*Your legal rights:*
1. Under Section 350 & 351 PPC, using force without consent is criminal force and assault.
2. Under Section 352 PPC, assault is punishable with imprisonment and fine.
3. If the slap caused visible swelling, cuts, bruised skin, or ear trauma, it is legally classified as "Hurt" (Shajjah) under Section 337 PPC, which is a serious punishable offence requiring financial Daman/Arsh.
4. Under Section 154 CrPC, if physical hurt is caused, police are required to register an FIR upon presentation of a Medico-Legal Certificate (MLC).
*What to do right now:*
1. Ensure your physical safety and note the assailant's appearance, vehicle make/model, and license plate number.
2. If there are visible marks, redness, pain, or bleeding, go immediately to the Emergency / Medico-Legal Department of the nearest District Headquarter (DHQ) or Government Teaching Hospital (e.g. JPMC / Civil Hospital Karachi, Mayo / Services Hospital Lahore, PIMS Islamabad) and get a Medico-Legal Certificate (MLC).
3. Check nearby shops, traffic junctions, or Safe City cameras for CCTV footage within 24 hours.
4. Go to the police station having territorial jurisdiction over the location and submit a written application (*Darkhwast*) detailing the assault.
5. If the police refuse to lodge an FIR, file a petition under Section 22-A CrPC before the Sessions Judge (Justice of the Peace).
*Exact script to say:*
"What you just did is a criminal assault under Section 351 and Section 337 of the Pakistan Penal Code. I am taking your vehicle details and proceeding to the hospital for an MLC and filing a police report."

## Scenario 6: Armed Robbery, Gunpoint Mugging, or Mobile Phone Snatching
**Category: theft_robbery**
*Common situation:* An armed robber on a motorbike or street corner stops you and snatches your mobile phone, wallet, or motorcycle at gunpoint.
*Stay calm rule:* Your life is more valuable than any property. Never physically resist armed attackers pointing a firearm. Hand over the property peacefully.
*Your legal rights:*
1. Under Section 392 & 397 PPC, robbery and armed robbery using a deadly weapon carry rigorous imprisonment ranging from 7 to 14 years.
2. Armed robbery is a serious, cognizable, and non-bailable crime. Police station is legally mandated under Section 154 CrPC to lodge an immediate FIR. They cannot dismiss it as a mere "lost report" (Gumshuda).
3. You have the right to get your phone's IMEI blocked through PTA DIRBS and tracked via CPLC / Police Cyber Cell.
*What to do right now:*
1. As soon as the robbers flee, dial Police Helpline 15 immediately to report the direction of fleeing suspects and vehicle description.
2. Find your phone's box or IMEI number (15-digit code).
3. Go immediately to the local police station and submit a written complaint for FIR registration under Section 397/392 PPC. Ensure you receive an official stamped FIR copy (*Naqal FIR*).
4. Block your stolen phone's IMEI through the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) via helpline 0800-55055 or cims.pta.gov.pk.
5. Immediately call your bank and block your SIM card, digital wallets (Easypaisa/JazzCash), and mobile banking apps.
*Exact script to say at Thana:*
"Officer, I was robbed at gunpoint, which is a cognizable offence under Section 397 and Section 392 PPC. I need an immediate FIR registered under Section 154 CrPC and a signed copy of the FIR so I can proceed with IMEI tracking and banking safety."
