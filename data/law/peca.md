# Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 (PECA)

## Section 3: Unauthorized access to information system or data
**Category: cyber, general**
Whoever with dishonest intention gains unauthorized access to any information system or data shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three months or with fine which may extend to fifty thousand rupees or with both.
*Practical legal implication:* Hacking someone's WhatsApp, email, Instagram, or mobile phone without permission is an offence under PECA Section 3.

## Section 20: Offences against dignity of a natural person (Online Defamation and Libel)
**Category: cyber, women, general**
Whoever intentionally and publicly exhibits or displays or transmits any information through any information system, which he knows to be false, and intimidates or harms the reputation or privacy of a natural person, shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years or with fine which may extend to one million rupees or with both.
*Practical legal implication:* Creating fake accounts, spreading doctored images, fabricated rumors, or defamatory posts on social media or group chats is punishable under Section 20.

## Section 21: Offences against modesty of a natural person and minor (Non-consensual imagery & blackmail)
**Category: cyber, women, general**
Whoever intentionally and publicly exhibits or displays or transmits or publishes or distributes through any information system any video or photograph of any person in a sexually explicit act or nude or partially nude, without the express consent of the person involved, or superimposes a photograph of the face of any person on any sexually explicit image, or blackmails any person with such image or video, shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to five years or with fine which may extend to five million rupees or with both; and if the victim is a minor, with imprisonment for a term which may extend to seven years and fine up to five million rupees.
*Practical legal implication:* Blackmailing anyone with private pictures, naked photos, edited deepfakes, or intimate video clips is a severe non-bailable crime investigated by FIA NR3C. Victims are strongly advised never to pay the extortionist, as paying increases demands; instead preserve screenshots and report immediately to FIA 1991.

## Section 24: Cyberstalking
**Category: cyber, women, general**
Whoever with the intent to coerce, intimidate, or harass any person uses information system, website, electronic mail, or any other similar means of communication to:
(a) follow a person or repeatedly communicate with such person;
(b) monitor the use by a person of the internet, email, text messaging, or any other electronic communication;
(c) watch or spy upon a person in such manner as causes intimidation, fear, or mental anguish; or
(d) take pictures or video of any person without consent and distribute the same;
shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to three years or with fine which may extend to one million rupees or with both.
*Practical legal implication:* Persistent unwanted messaging on WhatsApp, stalking across Instagram/Facebook, threatening calls via virtual numbers, or tracking someone's online activity without consent constitutes criminal cyberstalking.
