# Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (CrPC)

## Section 154: Information in cognizable cases (First Information Report - FIR)
**Category: police, theft_robbery, assault, general**
Every information relating to the commission of a cognizable offence (such as robbery under Section 392 PPC, armed robbery under Section 397 PPC, or grievous hurt under Section 337 PPC) given to an officer in charge of a police station shall be reduced to writing and entered in the official register (Book 154).
*Practical legal implication:* In serious cognizable offences like mobile phone snatching, robbery, or severe physical attack, the police station (Thana) is legally bound to register an FIR without delay. Police CANNOT refuse by giving a simple slip or asking you to wait days. If an officer refuses, you have legal remedies under Section 22-A CrPC.

## Section 22-A & 22-B: Powers of Justice of the Peace (When police refuse FIR)
**Category: police, theft_robbery, assault, general**
An ex-officio Justice of the Peace (the District & Sessions Judge or Additional Sessions Judge) has the legal authority upon an application by an aggrieved citizen to direct the police to register a criminal case (FIR) under Section 154 CrPC, or to take legal action against negligent police officers.
*Practical legal implication:* If the police station refuses to register an FIR for your robbed mobile phone, snatched purse, or physical assault, you can immediately file an application under Section 22-A / 22-B CrPC before the Sessions Judge (Justice of the Peace) in your district.

## Section 155: Information in non-cognizable cases (Daily Diary / Roznamcha)
**Category: assault, general**
When information is given to an officer in charge of a police station of the commission within the limits of such station of a non-cognizable offence (such as simple abuse under Section 504 PPC or simple assault without visible injury under Section 352 PPC), he shall enter the substance of the information in the Daily Diary (Roznamcha) and refer the informant to the Magistrate.
*Practical legal implication:* If a minor scuffle or slap resulted in no visible injury, police register a Roznamcha entry (report) rather than an automatic FIR. The victim can approach the Area Judicial Magistrate to initiate proceedings.

## Section 160: Police officer's power to require attendance of witnesses
**Category: police, general**
Any police officer making an investigation may, by order in writing, require the attendance before himself of any person acquainted with the case. Summons must be in writing. Women and minors under fifteen CANNOT be required to attend any place other than their residence.
*Practical legal implication:* A nighttime phone call or WhatsApp message ordering you to report to a police station is not a valid summons. Summons must be in writing and signed.

## Section 54: When police may arrest without warrant
**Category: police, general**
Police may arrest without warrant only for cognizable offences or upon credible complaint/suspicion. In non-cognizable disputes, arrest without a magistrate's warrant is illegal.

## Section 61 & 167: Maximum 24-hour detention & Remand
**Category: police, general**
No arrested person can be detained for longer than 24 hours without being produced before an Area Magistrate. Remand in police custody requires judicial sanction and cannot be granted arbitrarily.
